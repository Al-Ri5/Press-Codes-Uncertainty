# Press Code Themes & Subthemes for Communicating and Managing Uncertainty

## Main methods for communicating epistemic uncertainty
The reader-facing uncertainty-communication methods are:

### Visible separation of fact, opinion and conjecture:
- Readers must be able to recognise clearly whether an item is a factual report, the reproduction of third-party views, or the journalist's own comment.
- Facts must be clearly and unequivocally distinguished for the public from suppositions, allegations, interpretations, conjecture and fictitious material.


### Labelling unconfirmed, doubtful and unproven information:
- Unconfirmed reports, rumours, speculation and unverified information must always be explicitly indicated as such to the audience.
- Unproven claims — early research findings, experimental treatments, fortune-telling and the like — must not be presented to the public as reliable.
- When doubtful information is published, the doubts and reservations must be conveyed and material from unreliable sources marked as such.


### Disclosing sources and provenance to the audience:
- Quotation marks may only signal verbatim reproduction, never the mere rendering of a statement's generic sense.
- Where anonymity or confidentiality is granted, this circumstance — and where possible how the source's reliability was assured — should be made known to the public.
- The sources of information, accusations and opinions must be named and referenced wherever possible and relevant.
- Material taken from other media, agencies, authors or social networks must be credited to its original source.
- The identity of those producing or preparing disseminated recommendations must be made known to the public.


### Marking edited, non-documentary and reconstructed material:
- Photomontages, retouched pictures and content-changing edits of images or sound must be clearly marked so that viewers and readers are not misled.
- Reconstructions, stagings and acted-out events must be clearly identified as such to the audience.
- Archive, symbolic, stock and illustrative material must be labelled whenever it could otherwise be taken as documentary or misleading.
- When third-party recommendations are essentially modified, the extent of the modification must be revealed.


### Publishing visible corrections, updates and revision histories:
- Incorrect published statements must be voluntarily, promptly and explicitly corrected before the audience.
- Justified requests for rectification and opportunities to counter inaccuracies must be honoured and published.
- Earlier reporting must be updated for the audience — court outcomes, acquittals, exonerations and essential new findings must be published with due emphasis.
- Corrections must be published with due prominence, in equivalent placement and form, so that they reach those who received the original information, with an apology where necessary.
- Online corrections must be marked as such, linked to the original item, and must show what was wrong and how and when the content was changed.


### Granting visible reply and airing contested views:
- When no response could be obtained from an accused or criticised party, the public must be told of this in the report itself.
- Diverse and opposing views on contested matters must be presented so the audience can see the contested nature of the information.
- Those affected by accusations or criticism must be given a fair, timely and prominently published opportunity to reply, preferably in the same publication.

### Declaring interests, financing and independence conditions:
- Sponsored travel, external financing, payments for information and other support must be disclosed in the reporting.
- Personal, financial, ownership and organisational interests or conflicts that could affect credibility must be declared to the public.
- Interests and conflicts of interest surrounding investment recommendations — one's own and those of third parties — must be declared, or the public directed to where they can be found.


### Transparency about methods and production processes:
- Journalists should communicate clearly to the audience about their assignment, approach and choices.
- Automated news production, AI-based selection and personalisation of the news offered must be made clear to the user.
- The use of covert or exceptional gathering methods and the reasons for them must be disclosed and explained when publishing.
- Poll, research and testing reports must disclose the commissioner, methodology, sample and representativeness, or state that such information could not be obtained.
- Reporting produced under censorship or restrictions must be identified as such to the recipients.


### Making unresolved legal status visible to readers:
- The uncertainty of pending proceedings, the plea status and the non-final character of decisions must be made clear in the reporting.
- No person may be presented to the public as guilty or as a criminal before official confirmation of guilt.


### Demarcating journalism from advertising and non-editorial content:
- Advertising, paid and promotional content, official communications and other non-journalistic material must be clearly and recognisably distinguished from editorial content.
- Public forums on media websites must be clearly demarcated from editorial content.

## Main methods for managing epistemic uncertainty
The internal uncertainty-management methods are:

### Rigorous verification of facts before publication:
- Journalists must aim at maximum conscientiousness and accuracy, treating truth-seeking, research and consistent verification of facts as the prime duty of the profession.
- All information — including third-party statements, agency material, outside contributions and previously published items — must be checked as thoroughly as possible before publication.
- Urgency, immediacy or exclusivity must never exempt journalists from serious investigation and verification of facts and sources.
- Where information remains doubtful or limited despite reasonable efforts, journalists must exercise the utmost caution, avoid approximation, and judge whether publication with reservations is justified.
- Verification must draw on multiple, preferably independent sources, with cross-checking and corroboration of disputed or doubtful data.
- Reports should be supplemented as new information becomes available and news events pursued to the end to complete the evidential picture.

### Critical assessment of sources and their confidentiality:
- Journalists must judge when source anonymity or confidentiality is warranted, honour such commitments, and accept full responsibility for information obtained from unnamed sources.
- Sources must be treated critically, especially online material, PR material, pre-packaged content and statements that may be coloured by personal interest or intent to harm.
- Journalists must disseminate and report only information whose origin is known to them.
- Journalists must ensure that sources understand the conditions of publication and must withhold material when the sender's identity is in doubt.


### Hearing the accused and all involved parties:
- Before publishing charges or serious accusations, journalists must offer the person or institution concerned an adequate and timely opportunity to respond, or contact them afterwards if a prior hearing is impossible.
- Hearing the parties does not free the journalist from the duty to report as truthfully as possible and to remain critical of sources.
- In controversies and contested matters, journalists must seek out and hear all relevant sides and positions.


### Restricting assertions to sufficiently founded claims:
- Journalists must classify statements by their epistemic status, keeping facts strictly apart from suppositions, conjecture, rumour and unconfirmed reports.
- Unfounded, unproven or insufficiently substantiated suspicions and accusations must not be expressed or published, and the grounds of accusations must be investigated before use.
- Information known or reasonably believed to be untrue, distorted, misleading or incomplete must be refused publication.
- Heightened care is required for causal and sensitive claims — such as health, suicide and early research findings — which must not be overstated, oversimplified or presented beyond their evidential basis.
- Opinions and commentary must be grounded in a sufficient factual basis and must not distort, deny or conceal the facts.

### Restraint under the presumption of innocence:
- Journalists must respect the presumption of innocence, taking the uncertainty of pending legal proceedings into account and withholding any assertion or prejudgment of guilt until a final verdict.
- Later references to a case must take earlier judicial decisions into account, and unconfirmed past suspicions must be handled with restraint.


### Non-distorting editing, presentation and image handling:
- Quotes, interviews and contributions must be rendered faithfully; editing, truncation and authorisation must preserve meaning, tone and context.
- Images, sound and graphics must not be manipulated, fabricated or used in a misleading way, and their authenticity must be checked.
- Essential information must not be omitted, suppressed or altered.
- Material must not be reused or redistributed in a context different from the original in ways that give it a new slant or mislead.
- Headlines, captions, leads, trailers and other presentation elements must be substantiated by the content of the story and must not distort it.
- Reconstructions and staging may be used only when judged necessary and must reproduce the facts as faithfully as possible.


### Shielding accuracy from commercial and personal influence:
- Economic interests, inducements, paid sourcing, conflicting paid activities and disguised advertising must not distort or suppress editorial content.
- Journalists must maintain professional distance, avoid bias-inducing conflicts of interest, and disclose them internally or discontinue reporting.


### Producer accountability and internal error remediation:
- As soon as published information proves incorrect, the error must be acknowledged and corrected promptly on the journalist's or editor's own initiative.
- Evaluative reporting must meet generally accepted criteria and be produced by professionally qualified persons.
- Journalists bear personal responsibility for their statements, their work and the information they publish.
- Editorial organisations must maintain safeguards — including scrutiny of user-generated content and examination of complaints — to prevent the publication of inaccurate or misleading information.

### Editorial authorisation of exceptional newsgathering methods:
- Covert identity, hidden recording, disclosure of unverified information and other exceptional means may be used only for an important public interest that cannot otherwise be served, and only with authorisation at the highest editorial level.
